# Product: Read or Dread (working name)

A browser reading companion that helps people finish the book they uploaded.
The page is calm while you read. When you look away, the room slowly gets haunted.
When your eyes come back, the haunting retreats.

Built for Kiroween 2026, Costume Contest category.

## Who it is for

Adults and students who start books and do not finish them because they drift
to their phone or another tab.

## The one flow that must work

1. Upload a book (plain text or EPUB).
2. Read one paragraph at a time; click to open the next.
3. Look away from the screen: a haunt begins and escalates.
4. Look back: the haunt fades.
5. Finish the chapter: a character from it visits and you can talk with them
   (or answer one fill-in-the-blank check), then see session stats.

Anything that does not serve this flow waits until it works end to end.

## Objective

Help a reader finish the book they chose, by making the page the most
interesting thing in the room: calm while they read, haunted when they drift,
and rewarding when they finish a chapter.

## Promises we make

- The goal is finishing with focus. We never claim to make people read faster;
  research shows speed costs comprehension (Rayner et al., 2016).
- The camera feed never leaves the device. No accounts.
- Book text stays on the device, with one exception the reader must agree to:
  the chapter visitor sends the text read so far to an AI service.
- The app is fully usable with the camera off and with the visitor declined.
- Nothing interrupts the reader mid-chapter except a haunt for looking away.

## What we do not build

- Word-level eye tracking. Webcams are not accurate enough.
- Flashing one word at a time (RSVP).
- Characters that talk during reading, or animated acting-out of scenes.
- Any server piece other than the one function that calls the AI service.

## Tone

Playful gothic, not gore. A reading room at night: candle, paper, quiet.
A haunt is a nudge back to the page. A breach of focus costs a short penance
scene, which earned focus points can skip. Penance is brief and safe, never
cruel: the reader should want to come back tomorrow.
